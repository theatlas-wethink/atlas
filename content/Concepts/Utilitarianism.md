# Utilitarianism

**Utilitarianism** is a consequentialist ethical framework holding that the moral worth of an action is determined solely by its outcomes. Its core directive is the optimization of aggregate utility—maximizing overall well-being, pleasure, or preference-satisfaction while minimizing suffering.

---

## Core Principles

1. **Consequentialism**: Actions are not intrinsically right or wrong; they are judged exclusively by their downstream effects.
2. **Welfarism**: The primary measure of value is utility (well-being or happiness).
3. **Aggregation**: The overall moral value of a scenario is calculated by summing the individual utilities of all affected parties.
4. **Impartiality**: Every individual's utility counts equally in the calculation, with no weighted preference given to oneself, kin, or specific social classes.

---

## Primary Variants & Examples

### 1. Act Utilitarianism
Evaluates each specific action individually based on its immediate and long-term net utility. 
* *Mechanism:* "Does performing action $X$ in this specific instance produce more overall happiness than any available alternative?"
* **Example (Triage / Resource Allocation):** A emergency surgeon has five patients dying of distinct organ failures and one healthy walk-in patient. An extreme act-utilitarian calculation suggests sacrificing the single healthy individual to save five lives, as $5 > 1$ net lives preserved.

### 2. Rule Utilitarianism
Evaluates the utility of adhering to general rules of conduct rather than individual acts. An action is right if it conforms to a rule whose universal adoption maximizes total utility over time.
* *Mechanism:* "Does the general rule 'never break promises' or 'never harvest organs without consent' produce better net utility for society as a whole than allowing exceptions?"
* **Example (The Transplant Paradox Resolved):** Even though killing one patient saves five in an isolated moment, a rule allowing doctors to kill patients at will would collapse public trust in hospitals. Since a collapsed medical system causes vastly greater total net suffering, Rule Utilitarianism forbids the action.

### 3. Quantitative vs. Qualitative Utilitarianism
* **Quantitative (Bentham):** Focuses purely on the duration and intensity of pleasure (*Hedonic Calculus*). All pleasures are fundamentally equal in kind.
  * *Example:* "Push-pin is as good as poetry"—if playing a simple game produces the exact same quantity of pleasure units as reading Shakespeare, they are morally identical.
* **Qualitative (Mill):** Distinguishes between "higher" intellectual/moral pleasures and "lower" physical pleasures, arguing that higher pleasures carry intrinsic weight in utility calculations.
  * *Example:* "It is better to be a human being dissatisfied than a pig satisfied." The intellectual depth derived from literature or philosophy outweighs simple bodily satisfaction.

# Virtue Ethics

**Virtue Ethics** is a character-centered ethical framework. Unlike systems that define morality by downstream consequences or rigid duties, Virtue Ethics asks: *"What kind of person should I be?"* 

It holds that right action flows naturally from cultivating stable moral character traits (virtues) under the guidance of practical wisdom.

---

## Core Aristotelian Concepts

1. **Eudaimonia (Human Flourishing / Well-Being)**: The ultimate goal (*telos*) of human existence. It is not a fleeting emotion or pleasure, but a state of living well and fulfilling human function through excellence over a lifetime.
2. **Arete (Excellence / Virtue)**: A disposition of character that disposes an individual to act, feel, and think in ways that promote human flourishing.
3. **Phronesis (Practical Wisdom)**: The cognitive capacity to discern the right action in a specific context. Virtue is not blind instinct; it requires understanding *how*, *when*, and *why* to apply a virtue appropriately.

---

## The Doctrine of the Mean (Golden Mean)

Aristotle posited that virtue is typically a mean state between two vices: an **excess** and a **deficiency**.

| Vice of Deficiency | **Virtue (The Golden Mean)** | Vice of Excess |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Cowardice | **Courage** | Recklessness |
| Self-deprecation | **Truthfulness / Honesty** | Boastfulness |
| Stinginess | **Generosity** | Extravagance |
| Apathy | **Compassion** | Codependency / Intrusion |

* *Mechanism:* The "Mean" is not an exact mathematical midpoint. Courage in a trained soldier looks different than courage in a civilian; *Phronesis* determines where the appropriate mean lies for a specific agent in a given situation.

## Practical Example

**Scenario:** An employee discovers that their team leader is misrepresenting quarterly performance data to senior management.

* **Consequentialist/Utilitarian approach:** Calculates whether whistleblowing will produce more total benefit or harm for the team's jobs versus shareholders.
* **Deontological/Absolutist approach:** Applies a rigid rule against lying or breach of contract regardless of consequences.
* **Virtue Ethics approach:** Asks what a courageous, honest, and prudent person would do. A virtue ethicist uses *phronesis* to find a resolution—addressing the leader directly with honesty and tact rather than acting recklessly (public outrage without facts) or cowardice (remaining silent out of fear).

---

## Structural Critiques

* **The Action-Guidance Problem:** Offers broad character goals ("be brave") rather than clear decision procedures for complex moral dilemmas (e.g., bioethics, algorithmic alignment).
* **Cultural Relativity of Virtues:** What one society views as a virtue (e.g., military honor in Sparta), another might classify as a vice (e.g., aggression).
* **Self-Centeredness Objection:** Focuses heavily on the moral agent's own character and flourishing rather than prioritizing direct obligations to others.

---

## Structural Counter-Frameworks

* [[Utilitarianism]]: Judges actions purely on aggregate outcomes rather than the character of the actor.
* [[Moral Absolutism]]: Binds agents to universal, exceptionless rules regardless of individual character development or situational context.
* [[Moral Relativism]]: Challenges the existence of an objective standard for human flourishing (*Eudaimonia*).
